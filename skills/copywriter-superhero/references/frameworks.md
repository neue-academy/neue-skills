# Framework library

Read this after triage. The framework is chosen by **reader awareness**, not by habit —
that single variable decides more about whether copy converts than any word choice.

Default models pick one structure and apply it to everything, which is why their output
feels flat regardless of the brief. Matching structure to reader psychology is the whole
job.

---

## Step 1 — Awareness level (Eugene Schwartz's five stages)

Ask: **what does this reader already know?** Everything downstream follows.

| Stage | The reader… | Copy must open with | Never open with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Does not know they have the problem | The situation, dramatised. A story, a statistic, a scene | The product, the offer, or the price |
| **Problem-aware** | Feels the pain, does not know solutions exist | The pain, named more precisely than they could name it | Features, or "introducing" |
| **Solution-aware** | Knows solutions exist, not that yours does | The mechanism — why this approach works | Generic benefit claims they have heard |
| **Product-aware** | Knows your product, not convinced | Differentiation and proof. Head-to-head specifics | The problem. They lived it already |
| **Most aware** | Ready, needs a reason to act now | The offer and the deadline | Any education at all |

**Getting this wrong is the most expensive error in copywriting.** Explaining the problem
to a most-aware buyer insults them. Leading with price to an unaware reader loses them in
one line.

## Step 2 — Traffic temperature

| Temperature | Who | Structural consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold** | Never heard of you. Ads, SEO, cold email | Full build: earn attention, establish credibility before any ask |
| **Warm** | Knows you. List, retargeting, social following | Skip the education. Open at the problem or the mechanism |
| **Hot** | Ready to buy. Cart, pricing page, renewal | Offer and remove friction. Nothing else |

Cold traffic with a warm-traffic structure reads as presumptuous. Hot traffic with a
cold-traffic structure reads as stalling.

## Step 3 — Pick the framework

| Framework | Stages | Best match |
|---|---|---|
| **AIDA** | Attention → Interest → Desire → Action | Cold or unaware audience. Ads, hero sections, cold email |
| **PAS** | Problem → Agitate → Solution | Problem-aware. Fast conversion, retargeting, short ads |
| **BAB** | Before → After → Bridge | Solution-aware. Transformation offers, SaaS, coaching |
| **FAB** | Features → Advantages → Benefits | Product-aware. Technical or spec-heavy products, comparisons |
| **4Ps** | Promise → Picture → Proof → Push | Direct-response landing pages and offer pages |
| **PASTOR** | Problem → Amplify → Story → Testimony → Offer → Response | Long-form VSLs, authority launches, warm-but-sceptical leads |
| **StoryBrand (SB7)** | Character → Problem → Guide → Plan → Action → Success/Failure | Full websites, brand positioning, narrative funnels |

### Decision shortcut

```
Unaware        → AIDA        (earn the attention first)
Problem-aware  → PAS         (name the pain, then twist, then release)
Solution-aware → BAB         (show the gap, then bridge it)
Product-aware  → FAB or 4Ps  (differentiate on specifics, then prove)
Most aware     → offer + deadline. No framework needed
Long + sceptical → PASTOR    (story and testimony carry the weight)
Whole website  → SB7         (the reader is the hero, you are the guide)
```

## What each stage must actually contain

Stage names are useless without knowing what fills them.

### AIDA
- **Attention** — a specific, checkable claim or an unexpected fact. Not a question.
- **Interest** — relevance to *this* reader: their situation, in their words.
- **Desire** — the outcome made concrete, plus proof it is achievable.
- **Action** — one imperative ask, with the friction named and removed.

### PAS
- **Problem** — one specific moment, not a mood. "The fourth rebuild this quarter."
- **Agitate** — the second-order cost: what the problem causes. Money, time, standing.
  Agitate with consequence, never with insult.
- **Solution** — the mechanism, then the ask. Not "we can help" but *how*.

### BAB
- **Before** — the reader's world today, in specifics they recognise.
- **After** — the same world changed, with one number attached.
- **Bridge** — the thing that gets them across, named. This is where the product goes.

### FAB
- **Feature** — what it is. A fact, verifiable.
- **Advantage** — what that fact makes possible, mechanically.
- **Benefit** — what that means for this reader's day.
- Rule: never state a feature without walking it to a benefit. Never state a benefit
  without the feature that earns it.

### 4Ps
- **Promise** — the headline claim. Bold, specific, falsifiable.
- **Picture** — the reader living the outcome. Present tense, sensory, concrete.
- **Proof** — measured, then demonstrated, then asserted. In that order of strength.
- **Push** — the ask plus the reason to act now. The reason must be real.

### PASTOR
- **Problem** — the reader's situation, stated so precisely they feel recognised.
- **Amplify** — the cost of leaving it alone. Compound it over time.
- **Story** — how you or a customer found the way through. Specific, dated, humbling.
- **Testimony** — other people, other numbers. Attributed.
- **Offer** — the full stack: deliverables, price, terms, guarantee.
- **Response** — one clear action, repeated, with the risk removed.

### StoryBrand (SB7)
- **Character** — the reader, with one want.
- **Problem** — external (the thing), internal (how it feels), philosophical (why it is
  wrong). All three, briefly.
- **Guide** — you: empathy plus authority. Two sentences, not a biography.
- **Plan** — three steps. Never more, or it reads as work.
- **Action** — one direct CTA, one transitional CTA. Not five.
- **Success / Failure** — what life looks like both ways. Stakes make the plan matter.

## Framework does not mean formula

The framework decides **order and coverage** — what appears, and in what sequence. It
never decides sentences. Copy that visibly announces its framework ("Now let me agitate
this problem for you") has failed.

Two more rules:

1. **One framework per piece.** Stacking PAS inside AIDA inside SB7 produces copy that
   restarts three times.
2. **State the framework in the triage block**, then never mention it again in the
   deliverable. The reader should feel structure, not see it.

## Angles are orthogonal to frameworks

A framework is the **spine**. Angles are the **argument** you run along it. The same PAS
structure can carry a Logic angle, a Pain angle, or a Social Proof angle, and the linter
requires three distinct angles for short-form work regardless of which framework you
chose. Recipes: [funnel-angle-recipes.md](funnel-angle-recipes.md).
