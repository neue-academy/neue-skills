# Funnel angle recipes

Three angles is a structural requirement because a single draft gives the reader
nothing to choose between and gives you nothing to test. These are concrete anchors,
not abstract categories — copy the shape, replace the specifics.

Every example below is written for Neue Academy's real offer (30+ project breakdowns,
20+ industry-grade tools, production source code, founding-member rate locked at beta).
Swap in the actual offer from the brief.

---

## Angle 1 — Logic

**Leads with:** arithmetic the reader can check. Time, money, or output, counted.
**Fails when:** the number is invented, or it is a percentage with no denominator.

### Recipe

1. State the unit the reader already spends (hours, dollars, attempts).
2. State what it converts into here.
3. Show the arithmetic in one line.
4. CTA that references the count.

### Worked example

> **Headline:** 30 builds, 20 tools, one subscription
>
> **Subhead:** A single studio project quoted at €4,000 takes about 60 hours to
> reverse-engineer alone. There are 30 of them inside, already broken down.
>
> **Body:** Every breakdown ships with the production repo — the same files that went
> to the client, not a stripped teaching version. You read the commit history, see
> which decisions got reverted, and copy the parts that apply to your own work. That is
> where the 60 hours goes: not in watching, in reconstructing.
>
> **CTA:** Lock the founding rate

**Why it works:** the claim is checkable, the mechanism is named (production repo,
commit history), and the closing sentence does arithmetic instead of restating.

### Openers that work

- `[N] builds, [N] tools, one [unit]`
- `[Task] takes [N hours] alone. Here it takes [N].`
- `The maths on [offer]: [input] in, [output] out.`
- `[Price] once. [N] deliverables. Do the division.`

## Angle 2 — Pain

**Leads with:** the cost of doing nothing, named as a specific event, not a feeling.
**Fails when:** it opens with a question ("Tired of X?") or describes a mood instead of
a moment.

### Recipe

1. Name one concrete moment of friction the ICP had this month.
2. Name what it cost, in their unit.
3. Name the reason it keeps happening (the mechanism of the pain).
4. Show the mechanism that removes it.
5. CTA that references stopping the loop.

### Worked example

> **Headline:** You have rebuilt the same hero section four times this year
>
> **Subhead:** Different client, same three days lost to spacing, scroll timing and a
> reveal that stutters on Safari.
>
> **Body:** Nobody hands you the file that already solved it. Tutorials stop at the
> pretty part, and the studios that got it right are not publishing their repos. So the
> fourth rebuild costs what the first one did. Every breakdown here ends at the
> deployed URL, with the easing values and the reduced-motion fallback still in the
> file.
>
> **CTA:** Stop rebuilding from scratch

**Why it works:** "four times this year", "three days", "stutters on Safari" are
photographable. The pain has a named cause, and the fix is a named artefact.

### Openers that work

- `You have [specific repeated action] [N] times this [period]`
- `[Day of week], [time]: [the exact moment it breaks]`
- `The [N]th [task] costs the same as the first. That is the problem.`
- `[Concrete artefact] is where the [N hours] goes.`

**Never open Pain with:** "Are you tired of…", "Struggling with…", "We all know
that…", "Let's be honest".

## Angle 3 — Social Proof

**Leads with:** a person like the reader who already did it, and what changed.
**Fails when:** the proof is unattributed, or the testimonial praises the product
instead of reporting a result.

### Recipe

1. Name the person or the cohort as specifically as permission allows.
2. State where they started, in the reader's terms.
3. State the result, with a number and a timeframe.
4. Name the mechanism they used — so the reader sees a path, not a miracle.
5. CTA that references joining them.

### Worked example

> **Headline:** The first 200 seats went to people who had never deployed anything
>
> **Subhead:** Founding members shipped their first live project inside the private
> sandbox before the public launch date.
>
> **Body:** One of them had been quoting €600 static sites. He rebuilt his own
> portfolio from the scroll-timeline breakdown, put the deployed URL at the top of it,
> and repriced at €2,400 the same month. Nothing about that required new talent. It
> required seeing the finished file and the decisions behind it.
>
> **CTA:** Join the founding cohort

**Why it works:** cohort size, price before and after, the exact breakdown used. The
quote reports a result, it does not compliment the product.

### Openers that work

- `The first [N] [people] were [specific starting state]`
- `[Name] went from [before number] to [after number] in [timeframe]`
- `[N] people did this before [date]. Here is what the [Nth] one changed.`

**Proof strength, strongest first:** measured (number with a source) → demonstrated
(screenshot, repo, deployed URL) → asserted (a quote). If you only have asserted proof,
say so in the handover and keep it short. Never upgrade a quote into a statistic.

## Keeping angles separate

The linter blocks two angles sharing more than 50% of their content words. If it fires,
the angles are the same argument in different coats. Fix it structurally:

| Force | How |
|---|---|
| Different lead noun | Logic leads on a count, Pain on a moment, Proof on a person |
| Different proof | Never cite the same number twice across angles |
| Different verb register | Logic: *costs, converts, divides*. Pain: *breaks, stalls, repeats*. Proof: *shipped, repriced, moved* |
| Different CTA verb | *Lock* / *Stop* / *Join* |

## Optional angles

Use only when the brief asks for more than three.

- **Status** — what having it says about the reader. Needs a named audience whose
  opinion the reader cares about.
- **Contrarian** — attacks a belief the ICP holds. Needs real evidence; a contrarian
  angle without proof is just rude.
- **Curiosity** — withholds one specific thing. Only legitimate when the withheld thing
  is genuinely delivered on the other side of the click.
